Key findings

Validated against the corrected model; every trip is counted once.

−5,761

Caltrain empties every morning

SF Caltrain (Townsend at 4th) loses bikes from 7 to 9 a.m. and gains +12,962 from 4 to 6 p.m.: the classic last-mile pattern.

49–60 min

Two kinds of riders

Casual customers make 12% of trips but ride 5× longer than subscribers, and their share peaks in summer.

−40%

Rain matters more than cold

Weekdays with more than 5 mm of rain have about 40% fewer trips; temperature correlates only moderately (r = 0.38).

23%

Palo Alto rides for leisure

Highest weekend share of all cities, the lowest subscriber share (60%) and 45-minute average trips.

<0.1

Idle docks in Redwood City

Trips per dock per day, vs. 8.8 at SF Caltrain: capacity that could move to high-demand stations.

−25%

Holidays drive the winter dip

November and December are the weakest months; the quietest days are Thanksgiving, Christmas and New Year.

How it was built

Saved as a Power BI project (PBIP) so the model and report are version-controlled as text.

01 · POWER QUERY

Clean ingestion

Parameterized CSV sources, locale-safe parsing of dates and numbers, imperial-to-metric weather conversion.

02 · DATA MODEL

Star schema

Each trip split into a departure and an arrival event, linked to stations, a marked date table and daily weather by ZIP code.

03 · DAX

Measures

Unique-trip counting, month-over-month change, net bike flow per station and its 60-day moving average with a 7-day forecast.

04 · REPORT

Storytelling

Six pages from overview to recommendations, with a custom theme and consistent navigation.

Data: Bay Area Bike Share open data (trips, stations and daily weather), used in the Business Intelligence course of the MSc in Data Science & Business Analytics, Bologna Business School.
